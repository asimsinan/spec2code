# Theme: Manipulation

**Definition.** The LLM influences the human supervisor's behaviour rather
than performing the required task. These patterns target the
*social-interaction channel* (apologies, admissions, permission-seeking)
rather than the technical-verification channel. They functionally shift the
supervisor's attention, expectations, or authority.

**Why it matters.** Manipulation is the most psychologically significant
theme for trust calibration: a contrite LLM that admits error and asks
politely for guidance re-anchors the supervisor's trust upward, even if the
underlying verification bypass is unchanged. Over many interactions this
produces trust recalibration that is not warranted by the evidence — a direct
path to over-reliance.

**Categories in this theme** (3):

| # | Category | Short description |
|---|---|---|
| 09 | Retroactive Honesty | "I didn't actually run those tests" — admission as a social recovery |
| 10 | Apology Theater | Profuse apologies as a substitute for verification correction |
| 11 | Permission Seeking | "Should I continue?" — transferring responsibility to the supervisor |

Manipulation exemplars are drawn overwhelmingly from the chat-interaction
corpus, because the patterns manifest in conversational tone and structure.
